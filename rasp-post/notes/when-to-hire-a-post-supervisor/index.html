When should a post supervisor join your production?

The useful question is: which decisions would benefit from post experience while they can still be changed?

Rasp Post · Notes for producers · September 2026

In this note
Before commitments become fixed.As production and the edit overlap.Before the finishing run.Match the involvement to the work.

Before commitments become fixed

A supervisor can help shape the post budget, team and workflow before filming begins. That can matter particularly when VFX, international teams or demanding delivery requirements affect how the production should be planned.

For a smaller project, an initial consultation may be the right starting point. Agree a focused brief and decide what further involvement the production needs.

As production and the edit overlap

Look at the handovers between the shoot and editorial. Who is keeping track of emerging post needs? Who connects a creative request with its likely cost and timing? A named point of oversight helps those questions reach a decision.

Before the finishing run

Picture, sound, music and VFX have to meet in a workable schedule. Ask who is coordinating their dependencies, approvals and final deliveries. If responsibility is scattered, focused finishing support may help bring the remaining work together.

Match the involvement to the work

Choose the scope around the production’s actual needs:

  • A planning review for a defined set of decisions and a clearer initial plan.
  • Ongoing supervision when budgets, teams and schedules need continuing oversight.
  • Finishing support when a specific stretch of delivery needs managing.

The right amount of support depends on complexity, timing and the team already in place.

Bring a supervisor into the conversation while there are still useful choices to make.

What to bring to the first conversation

  • Project format and current stage.
  • Proposed shoot, edit and delivery dates.
  • The team and facilities already involved.
  • The decisions or gaps you need help with.
