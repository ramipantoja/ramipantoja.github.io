What to decide before you lock your post budget

A post budget is a set of assumptions about how the film will be finished. Make those assumptions visible before you commit to the number.

Rasp Post · Notes for producers · September 2026

In this note

Start at the delivery end

Confirm who is receiving the finished work, what they expect and when. Include the required versions, supporting materials and time to address quality-control notes. Work backwards from those commitments when shaping the schedule.

Ask what the quote assumes

Give facilities the same brief. Record the included days, versions, review rounds and exclusions. A lower headline price is hard to assess when it covers a different amount of work.

Put unresolved assumptions beside the figures. An allowance still waiting for a creative decision should be easy to spot.

Put decisions in the diary

Identify who approves the cut, VFX, music, grade and mix. Give them realistic review windows. If a key person is unavailable, the impact belongs in the schedule before sessions are booked.

Work through one awkward scenario

Ask the team: “What changes if picture lock moves?” List the bookings, people and recurring costs affected. Then decide which commitments can move and which need protection. This makes the contingency conversation specific.

Give the plan an owner

Agree who keeps the budget and schedule current, who can approve additional spend and how changes reach the team. Set a regular review point so the working documents remain useful.

Before approval, aim for a budget, schedule and short assumptions list that tell the same story.

Before you approve the plan

  • Delivery requirements and dates are understood.
  • Facility quotes can be compared on the same basis.
  • Review windows and decision-makers are named.
  • Unresolved assumptions have an owner and a decision date.
